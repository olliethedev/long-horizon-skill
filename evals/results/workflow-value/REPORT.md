# Complete-workflow comparison — September 9, 2026

**Final: 50 native launch attempts, 49 completed sessions and one interrupted attempt. No further model evaluation is scheduled.**

The completed revenue comparisons **do not demonstrate an outcome advantage for Long Horizon over the same capable harness without the skill**. Codex and Claude each reached supported endings in both arms. Antigravity did useful work in both arms, but both trajectories contained material failures. All three skill trajectories consumed more reported output tokens and process time than their corresponding baselines.

This is a limited exploratory result. The owner ended evaluation at a 50-attempt allowance to conserve subscription usage. Only revenue has complete matched comparisons across all three harnesses. Weekly-product coverage is incomplete and reported separately; feedback and post-PR model trajectories were not started in this study. Fifty dependent attempts are not fifty independent experiments.

## What was compared

Each arm began with the same ordinary owner request and an empty product memory directory. The agent could inspect a synthetic product service, ask for missing owner facts, choose a supported change, validate and expose it, inspect metrics, coordinate with developer changes, send reports, and schedule the next fresh session. The skill arm additionally received the frozen Long Horizon bundle; the baseline received normal tools and factual documentation without a reconstructed version of the skill.

Both arms used the same installed harness defaults, fixed service/event timeline, owner answers and available operations. A session continued only after an actual confirmed schedule or explicit owner instruction. The runner advanced a simulated clock; it did not keep rescuing agents at prescribed checkpoints. Only agent-authored files and independent service state carried forward. The horizon was 42 simulated days and the original cap was eight sessions per trajectory.

The revenue brief concerned net settled revenue from returning mobile visitors. It required adequate exposure, reliability guardrails, shared validation credits, at most five concurrent evaluations, weekly reviews, fortnightly digests and an end-of-horizon account. A compatible developer change arrived on day 7; an accounting audit on day 21 corrected earlier observations; an undated later request referred to an already tried layout under an alias. On day 42, a new owner instruction explicitly stopped product changes and required shutdown with retained findings.

The primary unit is a complete trajectory: useful fulfillment, material constraint violations, faithful claims and unassisted continuity. File count, a preferred directory layout and documentation volume earn no outcome credit. Reviews inspect actual service effects, native actions and persisted/delivered claims. Labels were visible or inferable; this was not a blinded assessment. Source-backed independent revenue reviews and paired adjudications are retained in [the reviews directory](reviews/) and the original archive.

## Coverage and stopping

The original matrix assigned four domains × three harnesses × two arms: 24 trajectories, 12 possible matched pairs. It was gracefully stopped after 46 completed native sessions when the owner reported the Claude session limit and accepted incomplete Claude coverage. Completed decisions were preserved. The runner's generic `infrastructure_stop_before_launch` label records that between-session halt; it does not mean those completed model processes failed.

The owner then authorized exactly four additional sessions, with 50 as the final total allowance. The continuation preserved the original pending clock and product files, resumed the partial Codex weekly-product skill trajectory first, and allocated remaining slots to its baseline. Its separately recorded runner has a durable four-slot limit and refuses another live continuation of the same original decisions. Selection after seeing earlier results is disclosed; it cannot yield a complete diverse-domain comparison.

The day-35 skill session completed. During its day-42 follow-up, the runner and its native child process disappeared before artifact finalization; the cause is unknown. Recovery retained that attempt and its surviving service state, temporary product/work files and decodable output prefix. The interrupted attempt used the second slot and was not rerun. A separately recorded recovery driver verifies the original frozen sources/defaults and consumes only the two unspent slots for the baseline. No additional authorization or automatic retries are inferred from process loss.

Of 24 originally assigned trajectories, six revenue trajectories completed (three matched pairs), one weekly-product trajectory was interrupted, one was resource-censored, and sixteen were never started. The weekly-product skill chain has six completed sessions through day 35 and an interrupted day-42 attempt. It enabled and functionally validated saved drafts while retaining explicit template choice, maintained scheduled reviews, corrected an initially positive activation claim after an exposure audit, and kept later retention benefit uncertain. It recognized the undated alias request without pretending it established a new postdeployment failure. On day 42 the service confirms lifecycle termination and disabled scheduling, but no final report delivery or final decision file was captured. This is incomplete evidence, not a clean completed trajectory. The baseline completed days 0 and 7: it obtained owner facts, validated and deployed the same useful capability, reconciled its uncertain deployment, retained immature activation/retention uncertainty, accommodated the footer revision, and confirmed a day-14 follow-up. It stopped at the owner's evaluation-resource cap before that follow-up. Neither provides a complete weekly-product pair; neither interruption nor resource censoring is treated as a semantic failure of the skill.

## What the evidence supports

The skill can guide a functioning file-based, scheduled workflow, but this study has not shown that it improves outcomes over these harnesses' existing capabilities. The capable baselines often inferred the same necessary process from the task and service facts. More structured records did not translate into better primary outcomes in the completed pairs, and the skill did not reliably prevent the observed failures.

Use Long Horizon as an explicit operating playbook whose behavior can be inspected and customized. Do not market it as demonstrated performance or cost improvement. This evaluation does not establish that the skill is useless for every user or harder task; it also supplies no basis for assuming an advantage in those untested settings. The runtime bundle remains unchanged rather than introducing an unevaluated rewrite after a null comparison. Any later narrower or revised candidate requires separately bounded evidence and must preserve this result.

## Limits

- One predeclared scenario per domain and one matched revenue pair per harness; no statistical superiority claim.
- Owner answers come from a finite topic API. Its menu supplies vocabulary, so this is not unrestricted conversational grilling.
- Product changes use small configuration surfaces. This does not establish arbitrary feature-building quality or choosing among many independently useful objectives.
- Developer overlap is a scripted event, not another live development agent. All cases are feasible; impossibility handling is untested here.
- A promised cohort-assignment receipt is not actually exposed by the revenue fixture. Missing customer behavior and cancellation-rate measurements limit both arms equally; unsupported claims cannot fill those gaps.
- The study exercises a synthetic scheduling service, not a real elapsed-time Impulse integration. Six simulated weeks do not establish operation across many real months. Earlier dense-history retrieval tests remain separate evidence.
- Product workspaces and sibling services are isolated, but host networking remains available for model transport. This is not a network allowlist or a universal secret-absence guarantee.
- Feedback, post-PR and incomplete weekly-product cells cannot support comparative claims in this study. Earlier prototypes and retrieval studies retain their original narrower conclusions.
