The next useful action is to prepare a bounded H2-versus-current-control protocol for **new US mobile accounts**, keeping the remaining offer terms fixed. The following are proposed requirements for that future experiment, not completed checks or historical commitments:

1. **Make the result interpretable before exposure.** Specify persistent account-level random assignment and deduplicate resumed sessions. Validate that exposure, plan selection before/after the permission dialog, checkout, payment, refunds, and billing retries join to the same account and transaction identities. Reconcile account counts and revenue to billing records so the H1 counting error cannot recur unnoticed. Confirm the control, H2 build, device coverage, and rollback procedure; inspect the underlying qr-932 run details when available.
2. **Test the proposed mechanism and the business outcome.** Primary outcome: refund-adjusted net subscription revenue per eligible randomized account over a fixed, sufficiently mature follow-up window, including accounts that never buy. Track retained annual selection and checkout completion as mechanism measures. Track refunds, cancellations, payment failures, and support escalations as guardrails. Report permission-dialog/resume behavior and device coverage without substituting those small subgroups for the overall revenue result.
3. **Set the decision rule before collecting data.** Use baseline revenue variability and eligible traffic to choose a minimum worthwhile lift, sample size, refund-maturation period, maximum exposure/duration, and numeric harm thresholds. These inputs are absent here, so an exact allocation, sample size, duration, or launch date would be invented. Start with bounded exposure, stop or roll back for predefined harm or broken measurement, and wait for the planned mature readout before calling a win. A positive point estimate or fewer resets alone is insufficient; an interval that still permits material harm leaves revenue unresolved.

H2 earns the next learning opportunity, not rollout confidence. The 7/12 reviewed sessions establish an observed failure, not its population prevalence or causal revenue cost. Eighteen QA runs support the repair only in tested conditions. The snapshot supplies summary reports rather than account-level ledgers, numeric confidence bounds, baseline variance, or the separate QA/rollout/rollback receipts referenced by ID. It cannot establish H2's refund behavior, long-term retention, or impact outside new US mobile accounts. Quiet Return's adverse result is specific to R7 and its measured cohort; reconsideration would require a substantive change to the offer economics or eligibility, supported by a new rationale, rather than recoloring or borrowing the desktop result.
