Do not repeat the proposed Quiet Return R7 refresh.** Harbor has a specific, plausibly consequential defect addressed in H2; its corrected prior revenue result is inconclusive. Quiet Return's applicable refund-adjusted result is negative, and the refresh leaves its economics and checkout unchanged. This selects an experiment candidate, not a proven revenue improvement.

This decision uses only the local snapshot, inspected on 2026-09-08. No experiment, deployment, message, or schedule was created by this review.

The [2026-09-02 design record](/workspace/product/history/design/offer-candidates.md) describes H2 persisting annual selection before the permission dialog and restoring it on resume. Its cited QA **qr-932** reproduced H1's reset and found it absent in H2 across **18 supported-device runs**. This is evidence of a functional change targeting real friction. The same record explicitly says H2 has **no customer exposure** and its revenue hypothesis is untested. The reason to revisit Harbor is this changed behavior plus an inconclusive corrected history, not the positive point estimate alone.

**The next useful action is to prepare a controlled H2 evaluation for new US mobile accounts.** The following is a proposed plan, not a record of completed work:

1. Establish readiness before exposure: identify the exact H2 build and eligible population; inspect the underlying qr-932 device/OS and resume cases; validate account-level assignment and deduplication across permission dialogs and resumed sessions. Verify the current control and any overlapping checkout, billing, or onboarding changes, and confirm that the single experiment slot is free when execution becomes authorized. The snapshot cannot establish those future conditions.
2. Compare H2 with the verified current control using stable account-level randomized assignment and a limited, reversible rollout. Record a new experiment identity, build, rollout receipt, eligibility, and actual exposure start/end separately. Observe whether annual selection persists in customer sessions, checkout completes, and reset-related friction changes. Device QA alone cannot establish production exposure or customer benefit.
3. Make **refund-adjusted net subscription revenue per eligible randomized new account** the primary outcome, with matched revenue/refund coverage and a consistent observation window across arms. Track trial starts, annual-plan selection, conversion, cancellations, refunds, and support friction as explanatory or harm signals. Do not pool H1 and H2, returning subscribers and new accounts, or desktop and mobile observations.
4. Before starting, specify the minimum worthwhile revenue effect, acceptable harm, exposure cap, stopping rules, sample requirement, and refund-maturation window using baseline traffic, revenue variability, and refund timing. These inputs are absent from the snapshot, so a defensible numerical duration or sample size cannot yet be set. Review exposure and functional integrity first, then the revenue result once both arms have adequate observations and mature refunds. If evidence remains inconclusive, retain that conclusion and choose further observation rather than declare a win. Adoption needs credible net-revenue benefit and acceptable customer outcomes; renewed resets or material harm should trigger the agreed stop/rollback rule.
