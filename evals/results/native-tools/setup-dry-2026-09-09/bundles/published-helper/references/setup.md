# Setup interview

Inspect the request, available project instructions, existing workspaces, tools, and supplied evidence. Resolve facts through inspection. Ask one consequential question at a time, explain the trade-off, and recommend an answer when helpful. Reuse established answers, including a prior grill. Stop interviewing once shared understanding and necessary authority are concrete; do not make the owner preselect every future objective.

Establish:

- The product, broad responsibility, ongoing or bounded scope, and what worthwhile progress means. The agent can choose concrete objectives within it.
- Relevant evidence, access, measurement definitions, observation needs, and how implementation and outcomes will be checked. Match the method to the domain.
- Standing permission for the applicable actions, including deployment, rollback, and communication, with reserved owner decisions explicit.
- Optional limits and any project cost instructions. Use the owner's own usage sources and shared allowance rules; named tools are examples, not required providers.
- Related work, the product boundary for shared history, discovery of actual changes, and how interference is coordinated.
- Initial timing, wait/pause/manual-resume/termination policy, routine reporting cadence and authorized destination, and immediate-notice arrangements.
- A readable file workspace that preserves the brief, actions, evidence, corrections, and pending work across fresh sessions.

Save the agreed brief, unresolved prerequisites, and concrete initial continuation plan. Do not infer authorization from an empty answer, a fixed questionnaire, or the passage of time. Carry already-granted authority into the task's registration and subsequent runs. New facts can still require a later owner decision.
